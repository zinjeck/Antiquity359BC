# Historical scope of the population revisions

## 0.3 additions

The supplied Germanic map depicts roughly 50 BC–AD 300, much later than the bookmark. Semnones, Hermunduri, Chatti, Marcomanni, Langobardi and smaller northern groups are backward projections, not securely documented 359 BC borders. The supplied pre-Slavic reference depicts hypothetical zones; river-basin labels are used without claiming contemporary tribal endonyms. Archaeological names such as Pazyryk do not establish a named political confederation.

Cadusian is used in the southern Caspian region instead of projecting the much later securely documented Daylamite identity over it. Persian is a people/culture; Achaemenid is a ruling house. Mashriqi, Sabaean and Bedouin follow the user's explicit Arabic-group convention.

Dahae, Massagetae and Saka locations draw on ancient-source scholarship but retain uncertain boundaries. Dahae westward movement is disputed; Yuezhi and Donghu evidence is stronger for later centuries. No unified Xiongnu empire is placed in 359 BC. Tagar communities are not universally made nomadic, and settled Persian provinces are excluded from blanket nomadic conversion.

China's state-based cultures model regional identities, not separate modern ethnic groups. Religious placement is simplified: a single county faith cannot fully represent overlapping household, local and ancestral cults. Existing religious mechanics are preserved.

## Original 0.2 revision

The scenario is still 359 BC. The user's 300 BC reference map was used to identify possible peoples, not to import the successor kingdoms or borders shown on it. The Fallen Eagle archive was inspected as a catalogue of later-antiquity culture and faith identifiers. No Fallen Eagle code, assets, province assignments, name lists or localization were copied.

County borders are CK3's map units and are necessarily coarser than ancient settlement patterns. Naming a county's culture models its population, not every resident, ruler or subject. Named rulers can retain a culture or faith different from the county; the Nanda ruler, for example, has a Vedic court faith while the capital county models an Ajivika community. This is a gameplay distinction, not a claim about a known numerical religious majority.

## Tribal names and chronology

Italy and Anatolia distinguish peoples such as Samnites, Umbrians, Messapians, Etruscans, Carians, Lycians, Lydians, Phrygians and Pisidians. Shared heritage or a related language does not merge those culture identities.

Many named British, Irish and Germanic tribes are securely documented only in much later sources. Their names and approximate territories are projected backward as scenario conventions, not asserted as proven 359 BC boundaries. This also applies to some Celtic groups around the Danube. Broad regional cultures remain where the revision lacks a defensible narrower identification. The old scenario's populated North Atlantic islands are retained as a gameplay placeholder, not an attested Iron Age settlement claim.

Several linguistic pillars describe reconstructed or poorly documented ancient groupings rather than fully recoverable spoken languages. A separate pillar is a gameplay abstraction; disputed classifications such as Mysian, Paphlagonian, Cappadocian, Ligurian and Paleo-Sardinian should not be read as settled linguistic taxonomy. Early regional Prakrit labels similarly approximate dialect continua.

## India and religion

Indo-Aryan is a language-family designation, not one uniform ancient people. This revision replaces that population umbrella with regional identities while retaining Indo-Aryan heritage where appropriate. Atavika is a descriptive forest-community category, not the name of a single established pan-Indian tribe. Associating some forest counties with early Munda and others with Dravidian traditions remains a reconstruction.

The five Vedic faiths represent Rigvedic, Black Yajurvedic, White Yajurvedic, Samavedic and Atharvavedic transmissions. These were overlapping textual and ritual traditions, not five mutually exclusive territorial churches. CK3 requires discrete county faiths, so their assignment is an abstraction. Later Buddhist and Jain sect labels are avoided for the new early communities.

Religio Romana, Disciplina Etrusca, Vaidika Dharma and the other labels distinguish religions from peoples. Where no single ancient religious endonym survives, a descriptive label is preferable to inventing one. Cult-centered faiths such as Matar, Artimu and Zeus Labraundos do not imply exclusive worship of one deity. Holy-site selections and tenet differences are gameplay approximations and should receive further historical review.

The revision does not claim to complete every region's religious, linguistic, clothing or personal-name history. It replaces the reported shared labels and large population umbrellas with a more detailed, editable starting model.
